What it does

More than four jobs.

A hundred-odd tools, grouped by what they do for a chat. Everything that spends, contacts someone or uses an account asks first.

Plans and books

  • Hotels and flights at wholesale rates
  • Restaurant tables and appointments, by phone when there is no form
  • Compares options on one shared page

Buys

  • Carts and checkout with a one-time card for the exact amount
  • Group orders from wholesale brands, split by person
  • Spending limits per owner

Coordinates

  • Who does what, with a name and a date
  • Polls the group, nudges one person
  • Reminders, follow-ups and watches
  • Shared checklists and pages

Reaches out

  • Calls and texts on the group's behalf
  • Email from its own address
  • Finds a person's number, with permission

Works with your apps

  • Google Calendar, Gmail, Drive, Sheets, Slides, Forms
  • Slack and WhatsApp summaries
  • Any MCP server or HTTP API you connect

Remembers

  • Per person and per chat
  • Your own chat never leaks into the group
  • Every reply shows what actually ran

Questions

Does everyone need an account?

No. One person owns Circle. Everyone else just talks to it in the chat. Anything that needs the owner's money or accounts becomes a card for the owner.

Why is the hotel cheaper?

Circle books through Nuitée, a wholesale network that sells to travel agents at net rates. It shows that rate next to Booking.com's total so you can check. No markup during the beta.

Can it spend money on its own?

Only under a limit the owner sets. The default is zero. Everything else is a yes-or-no card, paid with a single-use card for the exact amount.
